Per-project overrides for the Quick (draft), Turbo HD (fast HD draft) and Fine (keeper) render passes. Any of them can be swept across several seeds. Saved with the project.

refs only matters on a reference-to-video shot — one with media attached, or with a cast member on screen. match shrinks every identity file to that tier’s own canvas, so at 0.2 MP a face sheet arrives about 450px across; max sends it at a 2048px short edge. Reference tokens ride through every sampling step, so max costs real time. Reach for it when a face drifts and the prompt already says the right thing.

Turbo 2-pass spends Turbo HD’s same eight steps across two canvases — six composing at 0.2 MP, then two finishing at full size after the upscale. Same budget, different distribution: reach for it when a turbo draft keeps missing the direction. Its refs default to max because a two-pass tier’s match would size faces to pass 1’s small canvas; drop it to match on shots with no faces to get the time back.

Don’t move that split to 4. The upscaler needs a nearly-clean latent, and it is the sigma reached that decides that, not the step count — an eight-step run is still at 92% noise after four steps, and the upscaler magnifies the guess into speckle. Six reaches the same handover Fine 2-pass uses. Seven is clean but leaves too little to sharpen with.

Fine 2-pass composes at 0.2 MP — where H3 follows the prompt best — then upscales the clean latent and spends the remaining steps on texture at full size. Split is where the schedule is cut; the steps after it are the finishing pass, and more than a handful start re-inventing fine detail instead of sharpening it.

Project library

Reusable identities

Characters, locations, props and voices written once and reused. A member joins a segment three ways: named as a speaker on a line, ticked on screen below when they are present but silent, or linked from the Inputs tab's From cast. Any of the three sends their identity files, so a segment with a member on screen renders as reference-to-video whether or not the Inputs tab lists anything. Appearance, voice and identity files are folded in when the prompt composes, so editing a member here updates every segment naming them — use an Inputs tab link when one segment needs its own retention. Names stay in the editor — the prompt itself sees (S1) and <Picture 1>. Double-click a card — or press the button on its picture — to open a member's appearance, voice, notes and identity files.

Appearance, voice and identity-file edits apply across this project. On-screen selections apply to the current generation.

Source

Clip this cut only

H3 generates 5–15s, so short cuts are trimmed, never generated. Trim and speed belong to the clip — seconds into this source's lineage, its takes end to end — so they never touch the prompt, and a second clip on the same source can be trimmed differently. You can also drag the clip's edges on the timeline.

Playback sound

Sound is part of the clip, not a track of its own. A re-roll you rejected for its picture is the same lineage on the same clock, so choosing it here reads its audio over exactly the window the picture plays — trim the clip and the sound follows. Use Sync only if a borrowed take drifts.

DIRECTION

Build the scene

Scene foundation

The one or two sentences that come before [Shot 1]: the style first, the scene it opens on second. The camera line is not part of that opening — it is woven into shot 1, and only when shot 1 has no motion of its own.

Shot LoRAs

Use LoRAs for characters, clothing, motion or style. Add any required trigger words to Visual style below. Applies to every generation of this shot.

Only composes when two speakers share one shot — then it adds “Keep (S1) and (S2) visually distinct throughout…”. Untick it if that sentence reads as noise here.

Storyboard

Each becomes [Shot N] At MM:SS.mmm, …; only the start is emitted. Shots tile the clip end to end, so one shot's end is the next one's start — type into either and both move, and the shot on the other side of the cut gives up exactly what this one gains. Shot 1 starts at 0 and the last shot runs to the budget, so those two ends are shown but not editable. Fill each shot in its written order — composition → subjects, environment, action → camera — and give every shot a camera move of its own (type + amplitude + speed). A cut has to bring new information; if only the distance changes, move the camera instead of cutting.

A speaker key maps to (S1), (S2)… by first appearance across the whole generation; two keys on one line make a group (S1,S2). Only the words and the language go inside <d> — who is speaking, and how, stays outside it.

Frame text & constraints
Soundscape — diegetic

Ambient, physical-action and non-verbal human sound across the whole generation, as overall_soundscape. Not dialogue, not the score. Empty means H3 is left to invent the sound, so say something even if it is quiet.

Timed cues

Sound pinned to the same ruler as the shots, appended to the bed as at 6s: …. Leave End blank for an instant hit.

AtEnd SoundShot
Non-diegetic music

The score only the audience hears. Instrumentation, tempo, rhythm, dynamics — not what it makes you feel. Music a character can hear is a diegetic event and belongs in a shot instead. Empty composes to N/A.

Keyframes & references

Keyframes image-to-video

A first frame anchors where the clip starts; an optional last frame is where it resolves. Setting a keyframe renders this segment as image-to-video. Generate writes one from a description instead of uploading a file — the same picture can be reused anywhere, and every image you make is kept in the project's library.

Subjects what the video contains

H3 tracks subjects, not files. A person, a room or a prop is a <Subject N>; the pictures of them are only where it was cut from, and get cited inside its line rather than defined separately. Every cast member on screen who has identity files becomes a subject here on its own — edit the role only to change what this segment says about them. Add one by hand for something with no cast identity behind it.

References reference-to-video

Images, videos or audio the model should honour. Each gets a tag like <Picture 1>. The role becomes its own subject_definitions line — but only when the file stands alone, as a keyframe, a source video or an audio track. A picture a subject is cut from says nothing here: the subject speaks for it, and the row shows which one. Any reference renders this segment as the six-section reference-to-video document. From cast adds a member's identity files as rows so you can set the role and retention for this segment — those rows stay linked, so the file itself still follows the member. A member on screen also sends their files with no row here at all; adding one only takes over the wording.

What the references are for. Composed as the [...] prefix on the summary line, joined with “+”. Tick none and a sensible one is inferred.
